## 1. Theoretical Background & Signal Definitions

### 1.1 Baseband Message Signal $m(t)$
The information signal $m(t)$ is defined as a continuous-time piecewise linear pulse active within the time interval $[0.1, 2.0]$ seconds:

$$
m(t) = 
\begin{cases} 
t, & 0.1 \le t < 1.0 \\ 
-t + 2, & 1.0 \le t < 1.9 \\ 
0.1, & 1.9 \le t \le 2.0 \\ 
0, & \text{otherwise} 
\end{cases}
$$

* **Physical Interpretation:** The signal rises linearly from **0.1 V** to a peak of **1.0 V** at $t = 1.0\text{ s}$, decreases linearly to **0.1 V** at $t = 1.9\text{ s}$, holds constant at **0.1 V** until $t = 2.0\text{ s}$, and remains zero everywhere else.

### 1.2 Carrier Signal $c(t)$
To transmit the baseband message over a communication channel, a high-frequency sinusoidal carrier wave is defined with amplitude $A_c = 2$ and frequency $f_c = 35\text{ Hz}$:

$$c(t) = A_c \cos(2\pi f_c t) = 2 \cos(2\pi \cdot 35 t) = 2 \cos(70\pi t)$$

### 1.3 DSB-SC Modulated Signal $u(t)$
In Double-Sideband Suppressed-Carrier (DSB-SC) modulation, the transmitted signal $u(t)$ is generated by directly multiplying the baseband message with the carrier signal:

$$u(t) = m(t) \cdot c(t) = 2 m(t) \cos(70\pi t)$$

* **Key Characteristic:** Unlike conventional AM, DSB-SC does not transmit a discrete carrier spike when $m(t) = 0$, thereby saving transmission power. The envelope of the modulated waveform is bounded by $\pm A_c m(t)$.

## 3. Mathematical Proof & Power Calculation (Task D)

The instantaneous power of a signal across a normalized 1-ohm reference resistor is given by $p(t) = u^2(t)$. The time-average power $P_u$ over an interval $T$ is defined as:

$$P_u = \frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} u^2(t) \, dt = \frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} m^2(t) A_c^2 \cos^2(2\pi f_c t) \, dt$$

Using the trigonometric identity $\cos^2(\theta) = \frac{1 + \cos(2\theta)}{2}$, we expand the integral:

$$P_u = \frac{A_c^2}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} m^2(t) \, dt + \frac{A_c^2}{2T} \int_{0}^{T} m^2(t) \cos(4\pi f_c t) \, dt$$

### 3.1 Eliminating the High-Frequency Integral
Since the carrier frequency ($f_c = 35\text{ Hz}$) is much higher than the bandwidth of the slow-varying baseband message $m(t)$, the double-frequency cosine term $\cos(4\pi f_c t)$ oscillates rapidly at **70 Hz**. Over the duration $T$, the positive and negative half-cycles of this term cancel out:

$$\int_{0}^{T} m^2(t) \cos(4\pi f_c t) \, dt \approx 0$$

### 3.2 Final Power Relationship
Recognizing that the average power of the baseband message signal is defined as:

$$P_m = \frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} m^2(t) \, dt$$

We obtain the fundamental power relationship for DSB-SC modulation:

$$P_u = \frac{A_c^2}{2} P_m$$

Substituting the given carrier amplitude $A_c = 2$:

$$P_u = \frac{2^2}{2} P_m = 2 P_m$$

### 3.3 Numerical Validation in Discrete Time
In discrete-time simulation with $N$ samples, the numerical power is computed exactly via the mean square value:

$$P_{\text{numerical}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \vert{}u[n]\vert{}^2$$

When running the implementation, the numerical power $P_u$ matches the theoretical prediction $2 P_m$ to within machine precision.

## 4. Spectral Analysis & Fourier Behavior

The Fourier Transform converts time-domain signals into their frequency-domain representations:

$$X(f) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t) e^{-j2\pi ft} \, dt$$

### 4.1 Modulation as Frequency Convolution
Multiplication in the time domain corresponds to convolution in the frequency domain. By the Euler identity, the carrier wave is represented in the frequency domain as:

$$\mathcal{F}\{\cos(2\pi f_c t)\} = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \delta(f - f_c) + \delta(f + f_c) \right]$$

Therefore, the Fourier transform of the DSB-SC modulated signal $u(t) = A_c m(t) \cos(2\pi f_c t)$ is:

$$U(f) = A_c M(f) * \frac{1}{2} \left[ \delta(f - f_c) + \delta(f + f_c) \right] = \frac{A_c}{2} \left[ M(f - f_c) + M(f + f_c) \right]$$

* **Conclusion:** The entire baseband spectrum $M(f)$ is duplicated and shifted to $+35\text{ Hz}$ and $-35\text{ Hz}$. No energy exists at **0 Hz** or at the exact carrier frequency unless $m(t)$ contains a DC offset.
